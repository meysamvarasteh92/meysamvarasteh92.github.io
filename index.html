About

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, advised by Professor Robin Burke. My research focuses on interpretable and controllable recommender systems, particularly in multi-stakeholder environments.

I am interested in understanding why recommender systems make particular decisions, explaining those decisions to different stakeholders, and developing mechanisms that allow recommendation behavior to be meaningfully controlled. My current work spans provider-side transparency, counterfactual and comparative explanations, and controllable recommendation models.

01 · PROVIDER TRANSPARENCY
IntRS @ RecSys 2026

Why Didn't More People See It?

Recommendation Transparency for Providers

Developed a surrogate-modeling framework that approximates recommender-system exposure behavior and quantifies feature contributions to explain why content receives more or less exposure across the user population.

Surrogate Models · Exposure · Interpretability · Multi-Stakeholder Recommendation

02 · COUNTERFACTUAL EXPLANATIONS
IntRS @ RecSys 2026

Why This, Not That?

Pairwise Counterfactual Explanations for Recommender Systems

Developed methods to explain why one item is ranked above another by identifying interactions in a user's historical profile that contribute to the relative ranking of item pairs.

Counterfactuals · Ranking · Sequential Behavior · Explainability
Publication details
03 · COMPARATIVE EXPLANATIONS
IntRS @ RecSys 2024

Comparative Explanations for Recommendation

Research Directions

Developed a taxonomy of comparative explanation approaches and studied how comparison-based explanations can improve recommendation transparency across stakeholders, with particular emphasis on item and content providers.

Explainable AI · Comparative Explanations · Provider Perspective
