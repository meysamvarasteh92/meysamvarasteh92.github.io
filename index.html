About

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Colorado Boulder, advised by Professor Robin Burke. My research sits at the intersection of recommender systems, machine learning, interpretability, and controllability.

I focus on making ranking systems easier to understand and steer without sacrificing recommendation quality. My current work develops concept bottlenecks for Transformer-based sequential recommenders, studies provider-side exposure through interpretable surrogate models, and builds counterfactual explanations for comparative ranking decisions. I am particularly interested in research that connects rigorous ML methods with real-world personalization and multi-stakeholder recommendation problems.

Research

01 · INTERPRETABLE & CONTROLLABLE RECOMMENDATION
Target: WWW 2027

From Tokens to Concepts

A Post-hoc Concept Bottleneck for Interpretable and Steerable Recommendation

Developing concept bottleneck architectures for Transformer-based sequential recommendation that expose interpretable intermediate concepts while preserving 99% of the original ranking performance. Designing concept-level interventions and steering mechanisms, and evaluating fidelity, interpretability, and controllability across ML-1M, Last.fm, and Beer datasets.

Transformers · Concept Bottleneck Models · Steering · Sequential Recommendation · Ranking
02 · PROVIDER-SIDE TRANSPARENCY
IntRS @ RecSys 2026

Why Didn't More People See It?

Recommendation Transparency for Providers

Developed an interpretable surrogate framework for explaining system-wide item exposure using structural recommendation features. The surrogate achieves R² = 0.83–0.93 across 3 recommender models and 2 datasets, revealing model- and domain-specific drivers of exposure.

Provider Transparency · Exposure · Surrogate Models · Interpretability · Multi-Stakeholder Recommendation
Paper Publication details
03 · COUNTERFACTUAL EXPLANATIONS
IntRS @ RecSys 2026

Why This, Not That?

Mining User Profiles for Pair-wise Counterfactuals

Developed a comparative counterfactual framework answering “Why was item A ranked above item B?” by identifying influential interactions in user histories whose removal reverses the ranking. Evaluated across 3 datasets and 2 recommender models, outperforming established explanation baselines in most settings.

Counterfactual Explanations · Comparative Explanation · Ranking · Sequential Behavior
Paper Publication details
04 · RESPONSIBLE ML
Research manuscript

Fairness Auditing Through the Lens of Counterfactual Explanations

Effort-aware auditing of model outcomes

Developed a counterfactual-based fairness auditing framework to test whether sensitive groups require unequal effort to reach favorable model outcomes. Introduced qualification- and effort-based measures and evaluated fairness across multiple classifiers on Adult Income and Census Income using KL divergence.

Fairness · Counterfactual Explanations · Responsible ML · Explainable AI
